# Governance Pages

## Governance landing page

The page should immediately answer:

- Who can decide today?
- How are technical proposals made?
- How can someone contribute?
- How will governance evolve?
- Where are decisions recorded?

Opening message:

> Founder stewarded, maintainer reviewed, and publicly discussed.

This is more accurate than pretending a committee already exists.

## Governance card set

- Charter and Mission
- Principles
- Governance Model
- People and Maintainers
- RFC Process
- Contributing
- Funding and Conflicts
- Code of Conduct
- Security
- Licenses and Trademarks

## `/governance/model`

Required sections:

1. Current authority
2. Founder responsibilities and limits
3. Maintainer responsibilities
4. Routine versus material changes
5. Conflict handling
6. Appeals or reconsideration
7. Future committee transition criteria
8. Amendment process

## `/governance/maintainers`

Show:

- maintainer role definitions;
- current maintainers by project;
- nomination criteria;
- inactivity and removal process;
- conflict disclosure expectations;
- contact routes.

## `/governance/rfcs`

Registry controls:

- status;
- project;
- author;
- year;
- search.

Rows show:

- RFC number;
- title;
- status;
- affected project;
- review deadline;
- last update.

## `/governance/rfcs/[number]`

Page header:

- number;
- title;
- authors;
- status;
- dates;
- affected projects;
- discussion link;
- decision link when complete.

Body uses a standardized template documented in the RFC plan.

## `/governance/decisions`

Decision records complement RFCs. Not every decision requires an RFC, but material outcomes must have a public record.

The registry should show:

- ID;
- date;
- title;
- status;
- related RFC;
- decision maker;
- superseding record.

## Policy pages

Every policy page includes:

- current version;
- effective date;
- owner;
- last reviewed;
- next review target;
- plain-language summary;
- normative policy;
- revision history.

## Governance visuals

Use restrained diagrams and timelines. Avoid seals, gavels, government-building stock photos, or visual language that overstates legal formality.

The design can feel established through order, typography, and clear records rather than institutional cosplay.
