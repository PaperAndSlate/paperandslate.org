# Foundation Pages

## `/foundation`

Purpose: provide an editorial overview and route users to the foundation’s institutional information.

Sections:

1. What Paper & Slate is
2. Mission
3. Principles
4. People and maintainers
5. Governance
6. Funding and independence
7. Roadmap
8. Reports
9. Contact

Use real summaries and clear status. A card may be hidden if its page has no authoritative content yet.

## `/foundation/mission`

Required structure:

- mission statement;
- problem statement;
- who benefits;
- what Paper & Slate builds;
- what it refuses to become;
- definition of success;
- relationship between standards, tools, and future hosted services.

Recommended mission:

> Make educational systems easier to connect, understand, and improve through open, neutral, and durable infrastructure.

## `/foundation/principles`

Render the complete principle set from version-controlled content. Each principle includes:

- name;
- short statement;
- implications;
- examples of decisions it affects;
- related governance or RFC references.

The public principles page should be readable; the normative governance copy may be more formal.

## `/foundation/people`

Initial page structure:

- Founder and Steward
- Named Project Maintainers
- Contributors
- Advisors only when real
- Future governance roles
- How to become a maintainer

Person cards include:

- name;
- actual role;
- short bio;
- project scope;
- start date;
- approved links.

Do not use fabricated profiles, job titles, or stock avatars.

## `/foundation/funding`

Required launch content:

- current funding source;
- infrastructure support;
- whether anyone is paid;
- sponsorship acceptance policy;
- conflict and disclosure policy;
- commitment that normative standards remain freely accessible;
- current legal status.

Future additions:

- annual funding summaries;
- sponsor list;
- expense categories;
- grant disclosures.

## `/foundation/roadmap`

Use Now / Next / Later rather than precise dates for uncertain work.

Recommended categories:

- Foundation and governance
- File System
- Discovery
- Organization data
- Courses and standards
- Tooling
- Documentation
- Community
- Future Developer Platform

Every item includes:

- status;
- last update;
- linked project or issue;
- responsible maintainer where known;
- reason if blocked.

Roadmap content must not be treated as a promise.

## `/foundation/reports`

Archive annual reports and major transparency reports.

Before a full annual report exists, publish a page explaining the intended reporting model. Do not create fictional historical reports.

Annual report sections:

- year summary;
- project progress;
- releases;
- governance changes;
- contributors;
- funding and expenses;
- security events;
- corrections;
- next-year priorities.

## `/foundation/contact`

Separate contact paths:

- general questions;
- technical questions and GitHub Discussions;
- security reports;
- trademark questions;
- media inquiries;
- governance concerns.

Avoid exposing personal addresses. Use role aliases when email infrastructure exists.

## Shared page behavior

- Breadcrumbs are optional on top-level foundation subpages.
- Show last substantive update for governance-bearing content.
- Include a related-pages rail or closing card row.
- Use article-width prose rather than full-width long lines.
