# Roadmap, People, Funding, and Reports

## Roadmap

### Data source

Use a structured repository file, not freeform HTML. Roadmap items should be reviewable in pull requests and reusable on project pages.

### Horizons

- **Now** — active or immediately queued work
- **Next** — likely after current commitments
- **Later** — direction, not commitment

### Status

- Researching
- Planned
- In progress
- Blocked
- Complete
- Paused

### Presentation

Desktop may use grouped columns. Mobile should use a single list. Include a list/table view for accessibility and long-term scale.

### Honesty rule

Every item shows `updatedAt`. Items untouched for more than six months should be flagged for review.

## People

### Profile ownership

People data lives in a validated registry. Each person approves their bio, image, and links.

### Contribution recognition

Do not limit recognition to code. Support contribution types:

- specification authoring;
- implementation feedback;
- education-domain review;
- accessibility review;
- documentation;
- design;
- testing;
- governance;
- community support.

### Maintainer eligibility

The People page links to the public maintainer criteria rather than implying appointment is informal or personal.

## Funding

### Launch statement

Publish a transparent, short account of the actual situation. Example structure:

- Paper & Slate is currently funded by the founder.
- Standards and normative documentation are free.
- No organization receives governance authority through payment.
- Sponsorship rules will be published before accepting material sponsorship.
- Formal nonprofit incorporation may be considered later.

Replace the example with exact facts before launch.

### Future sponsor display

Sponsor logos must not dominate the homepage. Use the Funding page and annual report. Label in-kind support separately from cash funding.

## Reports

### Annual report template

1. Letter from the steward or committee chair
2. Mission progress
3. Project registry snapshot
4. Releases and compatibility changes
5. Governance and RFC activity
6. Contributor summary
7. Adoption and implementation evidence
8. Security and incident summary
9. Funding and expense disclosure
10. Corrections
11. Next-year priorities

### Metrics caution

Do not use GitHub stars as the primary measure of impact. Prefer implementations, contributors, specification reviews, citations, and successful interoperability examples.
