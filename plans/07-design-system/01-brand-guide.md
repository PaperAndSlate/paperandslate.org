# Paper & Slate Brand Guide

## Brand idea

Paper & Slate combines two durable educational materials:

- **Paper** represents readable knowledge, teaching material, records, and human authorship.
- **Slate** represents structure, public learning, revision, tools, and a stable technical foundation.

The identity should feel modern and technically capable without losing the seriousness and permanence of a classic educational institution.

## Logo concept

The icon uses two offset sheets:

- a slate-blue back sheet;
- a warm paper front sheet;
- a folded upper-right corner.

The overlap communicates interoperability, layers, and the relationship between human-readable material and structured infrastructure.

The wordmark uses a high-contrast editorial serif with a slate-blue ampersand. The descriptor is restrained tracked uppercase.

## Preferred lockups

### Horizontal

Primary for:

- website header;
- repository banners;
- documentation header;
- presentation title bars.

### Stacked

Use for:

- social profile images;
- posters;
- square or portrait brand panels;
- report covers.

### Icon only

Use for:

- favicon;
- app icon;
- compact mobile header;
- social avatar;
- decorative project attribution.

## Clear space

Define `x` as the width of the folded corner on the icon.

Minimum clear space:

- horizontal lockup: `1x` on all sides;
- stacked lockup: `1x` on all sides;
- icon: `0.75x` on all sides.

## Minimum size

Digital:

- icon: 20 px absolute minimum, 32 px preferred;
- horizontal: 180 px minimum;
- stacked: 150 px minimum.

Print minimums should be established after proofing with the final production files.

## Logo restrictions

Do not:

- recreate the wordmark with live type;
- change the ampersand color independently;
- add unapproved shadows or bevels;
- place texture inside the wordmark;
- stretch, skew, rotate, or rearrange the lockup;
- use the dark logo on insufficiently dark photography;
- use the light logo on pale linen;
- place the logo inside a generic academic seal and treat it as primary.

## Typography

### Display — Bodoni Moda

Use for:

- homepage and landing-page headlines;
- major article titles;
- selective numeric/editorial moments.

Weights:

- Regular 400
- Medium 500 when smaller or reversed

Guidance:

- short lines;
- tight but not negative tracking;
- avoid long body paragraphs;
- use optical sizing through the variable font.

### Interface and body — Inter

Use for:

- body copy;
- navigation;
- buttons;
- labels;
- tables;
- documentation prose;
- metadata.

Recommended weights: 400, 500, 600, 700.

### Code — IBM Plex Mono

Use for:

- code;
- file paths;
- field names;
- identifiers;
- terminal output.

Do not use monospaced type merely as decorative “developer” styling.

### Wordmark

The final logo is outlined custom artwork. It is not dependent on a web font.

## Iconography

Use Phosphor Icons.

- `regular` for navigation and controls;
- `duotone` for selected feature illustrations;
- `fill` only for compact selected states;
- maintain consistent 20 or 24 px optical size;
- avoid mixing unrelated icon families.

## Photography and illustration

Preferred:

- editorial still life;
- paper, slate, linen, books, documents, diagrams, pencils, and restrained technology;
- natural soft light;
- top-down or shallow three-quarter perspective;
- warm neutral backgrounds with slate accents;
- negative space for layout.

Avoid:

- generic stock students pointing at screens;
- graduation caps as the default education symbol;
- staged boardroom diversity imagery;
- glossy 3D SaaS illustrations;
- fake institutional seals;
- excessive chalkboard clichés.

## Texture

Linen/paper texture is a brand layer, not a page requirement.

- keep contrast below roughly 3–4% on reading surfaces;
- prefer CSS texture or optimized tiles;
- do not interfere with text contrast;
- remove texture behind dense tables and code;
- use stronger material texture in still-life images and section dividers.

## Voice

The visual voice is:

- measured;
- open;
- precise;
- durable;
- useful;
- quietly distinctive.
