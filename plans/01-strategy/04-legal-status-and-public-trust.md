# Legal Status and Public Trust

## Current representation

Paper & Slate may be presented as a mature, established initiative through high-quality design, transparent policies, versioned work, and public governance. It must not imply legal facts that do not exist.

Approved description:

> Paper & Slate is an independent open-foundation initiative building public educational infrastructure.

Avoid until incorporation:

- “registered nonprofit”;
- “501(c)(3)”;
- “charity”;
- “tax-deductible”;
- “nonprofit organization” if this would imply a legal entity;
- a fictional board of directors;
- annual reports containing invented financial statements.

## Foundation page disclosure

A concise disclosure belongs on the Funding or About page, not necessarily in the homepage hero:

> Paper & Slate currently operates as an independent open-source foundation initiative. Formal nonprofit incorporation may be considered as the contributor community and governance needs develop.

## Footer language

Use:

> © Paper & Slate contributors. Unless otherwise noted, documentation is available under CC BY 4.0 and code under Apache-2.0.

Do not write “Paper & Slate Foundation, Inc.” unless that entity exists.

## Funding page at launch

The Funding page should disclose:

- whether the founder personally funds infrastructure;
- any material sponsorships;
- whether contributors are paid;
- whether commercial entities have decision rights;
- the policy for accepting future sponsorship;
- the absence of paid access to standards.

A blank funding page reduces trust. Publish a short truthful statement even if funding is simple.

## People page

Use actual roles:

- Founder and Steward
- Project Maintainer
- Contributor
- Advisor, only with consent and real participation

Do not create vacant profiles with stock photography. Open positions can be listed as opportunities, not as people.

## Governance credibility

Trust should come from:

- explicit decision rights;
- public RFCs;
- named maintainers;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- archived decisions;
- clear licensing;
- security reporting;
- accurate project status;
- visible correction history.

## Trademark policy

The name and logo should not be placed under CC0 or Apache-2.0. Publish a permissive trademark policy that allows:

- truthful statements of compatibility;
- community-event references;
- unmodified logo use when linking to the foundation;
- forks to describe their origin.

It should prohibit:

- implying endorsement;
- confusingly similar project names;
- modified logos presented as official;
- claims of conformance without meeting published requirements.

## Privacy posture

The public site should collect as little data as practical:

- no authentication cookies;
- no advertising trackers;
- privacy-conscious analytics;
- newsletter email submitted only to Kit after consent;
- no sale or enrichment of subscriber data;
- clear retention and deletion instructions.

## Corrections

News and reports should support a visible correction note. Specifications should use commits and changelogs rather than silently rewriting released requirements.
