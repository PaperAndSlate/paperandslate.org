# Audiences and Positioning

## Core positioning

Paper & Slate is an open educational infrastructure foundation. It creates public standards, schemas, formats, conventions, and reference tooling that let schools, educators, developers, institutions, and public systems represent and exchange educational information more reliably.

The clearest positioning statement is:

> Paper & Slate is an open foundation building the common infrastructure education software and schools have been missing.

This statement should anchor the homepage, About page, social profiles, repository descriptions, and launch announcement.

## What Paper & Slate is

- A neutral home for open educational infrastructure.
- A standards and implementation initiative.
- A public governance and documentation system.
- A collection of independently versioned projects with common principles.
- A bridge between education-domain needs and implementable technical specifications.

## What Paper & Slate is not

- A school-management platform.
- A learning-management system.
- A commercial API company.
- A data broker.
- A replacement for government datasets.
- A certification body at launch.
- A registered nonprofit unless and until incorporated.
- A single file format pretending to solve every education problem.

## Audience hierarchy

### 1. Developers and implementers

Primary questions:

- What problem does each project solve?
- Is the specification stable enough to implement?
- What are the schemas, examples, and compatibility requirements?
- How do I validate, extend, or contribute?
- Who controls the standard?

Required site behavior:

- Technical substance appears above institutional marketing.
- Project maturity is impossible to miss.
- Documentation, schema, examples, source, changelog, and GitHub are one click away.
- Search understands exact property names and identifiers.

### 2. Educators

Primary questions:

- How does this improve teaching or reduce platform friction?
- Can materials remain readable and portable?
- Is this another technology mandate?
- Were educators involved?

Required site behavior:

- Explain outcomes in concrete language.
- Use educational examples rather than abstract data-platform examples only.
- Avoid presenting standards as something teachers must personally administer.
- Demonstrate respect for teacher ownership and workflow.

### 3. Schools and districts

Primary questions:

- What would implementation require?
- Does this expose private information?
- Can existing websites and systems adopt it incrementally?
- Is it vendor neutral and free?

Required site behavior:

- Separate deliberately published public information from private operational data.
- Publish security, privacy, provenance, and implementation guidance.
- Provide phased adoption paths.

### 4. Education software companies

Primary questions:

- Will adoption reduce integration cost?
- Is governance trustworthy?
- Are extensions supported?
- Is the license implementation-friendly?

Required site behavior:

- Publish compatibility and extension policies.
- Show reference libraries and conformance expectations.
- Make governance and patent-related license choices clear.

### 5. Government data organizations

Primary questions:

- How are identifiers, source history, updates, jurisdiction, and authority modeled?
- Can Paper & Slate coexist with official schemas?
- Does it preserve provenance rather than overwrite source meaning?

Required site behavior:

- Treat provenance, namespaces, mappings, and official identifiers as first-class concepts.
- Avoid claiming that Paper & Slate supersedes authoritative government definitions.

### 6. Researchers

Primary questions:

- Is the model reproducible and source-aware?
- Can datasets and versions be cited?
- Are assumptions and transformations documented?

Required site behavior:

- Preserve version, source SHA, release dates, and archived specifications.
- Publish methodological notes and data dictionaries.

### 7. Standards contributors

Primary questions:

- How are proposals made and decided?
- What work is open?
- What constitutes evidence?
- How is dissent recorded?

Required site behavior:

- Make RFCs, issues, maintainers, decision records, and project lifecycle visible.

### 8. General public and donors

Primary questions:

- Why does this matter?
- Who runs it?
- How is it funded?
- Is it credible and accountable?

Required site behavior:

- Provide plain-language mission, people, funding, reports, and contact pages.
- Do not make donation solicitations before a legitimate structure exists.

## Messaging hierarchy

### Level 1: one sentence

Open infrastructure for education.

### Level 2: homepage

Paper & Slate builds open standards, formats, schemas, and tools that help education software and schools work together.

### Level 3: project explanation

Each project defines one shared building block—documents, discovery, organizations, courses, standards, provenance, or tooling—and can be adopted independently.

### Level 4: technical proof

Specifications, examples, schemas, validators, libraries, changelogs, tests, and governance records.

## Differentiation

Paper & Slate should differentiate through:

- education-specific depth without vendor ownership;
- readable specifications paired with implementable artifacts;
- public governance from the beginning;
- independent project versioning;
- provenance and deliberate publication as core values;
- strong documentation and practical examples;
- open licensing with no planned monetization gate.
